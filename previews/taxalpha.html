Tax & Retirement

Tax Alpha

Imagine two friends who invest the same money in the same things and earn the same return. Years later, one of them has noticeably more. The difference isn't luck or skill — it's taxes. Every year, taxes on interest, dividends and gains take a small bite out of what an investment earns. That bite is called tax drag. Keeping the bite smaller, legally, is called Tax Alpha: extra money you keep without picking better investments.

Asset location

Put each investment in the type of account where it's taxed least.

Taxable accounts, tax-deferred accounts (like a Traditional 401(k)) and tax-free accounts (like a Roth IRA) are taxed differently. Deciding which investments live in which account can matter as much as what you own.

For example

Investments that pay out a lot of taxable income each year, such as many bond funds, are often held in tax-deferred accounts, while broad stock index funds may sit in a taxable account.

Keep in mind

It only helps if you have more than one type of account, and the best arrangement depends on your tax rates now and later.
